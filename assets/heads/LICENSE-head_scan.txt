What SilkCrowd changed
----------------------
`tools/prep_head.py` (reproducible, no external Python packages) converts the upstream
27 MB GLB into the 2.2 MB file in this directory:

  * keeps the geometry (9,273 vertices / 17,684 triangles) and the photogrammetry
    base-colour map, box-filtered from 4096^2 to 1024^2;
  * drops the tangent-space normal map and the displacement map: the scan's normals are
    already geometric at this density and the renderer does not use tangent-space maps;
  * fits the bust into SilkCrowd's head space using landmarks measured on the mesh itself
    (the neck is the narrowest slab between the shoulders and the jaw, the crown is the
    highest vertex, the menton is the lowest midline vertex in front of the neck, the skull
    width is the widest slab above the ears), then scales it uniformly so the skull is
    0.161 m wide and places the crown at y = 0.128 m. Nothing is reshaped or re-sculpted:
    it is a uniform scale and a translation.

The result is a *derivative* of the scan and remains under CC BY 3.0. If you redistribute
SilkCrowd or renders made with it, credit Lee Perry-Smith (Infinite-Realities, via
triplegangers) as the source of the head scan.

Regenerating
------------
    git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/BabylonJS/Assets /tmp/bjs
    python3 tools/prep_head.py /tmp/bjs/meshes/Lee-Perry-Smith-Head/head.glb \
        assets/heads/head_scan.glb --texture-size 1024

`--procedural-head` (or deleting this file) falls back to the parametric head, which needs
no binary asset at all.
